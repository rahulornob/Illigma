## Masks, layout grids, rulers and guides

- **Format** (`guides-grids.ts`, optional fields, no migration): `isMask` and `maskType` ("ALPHA" default, "VECTOR", "LUMINANCE") on any layer; `layoutGrids` on frames (Figma's GRID, COLUMNS, ROWS with count, gutter, offset, section size, alignment STRETCH/MIN/MAX/CENTER, colour, visible); `guides` on pages (`{ axis: "X" | "Y", offset }`). `layoutGridBands` lays grids out as Figma does.
- **Masks:** ⌃⌘M "Use as mask" (also in the context menus) groups the selection as "Mask group" with the bottom layer as the mask; with only masks selected it turns them back into layers. A visible mask hides itself and clips every sibling above it. The renderer clips by the outline (VECTOR) or composites with DstIn (ALPHA, LUMINANCE through a luma filter), inside a node and among a page's top-level pictures; a node with a mask child is never recorded in chunks. Hit-testing skips layers outside the masks below them. PNG and JPG export go through the renderer; SVG writes `<mask>` (alpha or luminance) and `<clipPath>` (vector); PDF clips by the outline, and draws alpha masks through the renderer when it can. Layers shows a mask icon; Properties has a Mask card (mask type).
- **Layout grids:** Properties "Layout grid" card on frames (add, type, show/hide, remove, count, alignment, width, margin or offset, gutter, size). Drawn on a 2D overlay canvas under the selection, never by the renderer or in exports. ⌃G (⌃⇧4 off macOS) shows or hides all grids (kept in this browser). Moves, resizes and drawing snap to column and row edges of the frames involved (snap lines in `snap/snap.ts` take part in edge snapping only, never equal spacing).
- **Rulers and guides:** ⇧R shows rulers along the top and left (1-2-5 ticks, the selection's extent tinted, its edges marked). Drag from a ruler to add a guide; drag a guide to move it, or onto a ruler to remove it; each is one undo step. Guides are snapped to always and draggable while rulers are shown. Code: `grids.ts`, `rulers.ts`, `useGridsAndRulers.ts`, `LayoutGridCard.tsx`, `masks.ts`; e2e `masks.mjs`.
- **Gaps:** no Figma design yet (cards, rulers and guide colour need design QA); grids aren't drawn or snapped on rotated frames; no per-grid colour picker; guides can't be selected and deleted with the keyboard; ⇧R and ⌃G have no View menu entries yet; masks inside auto layout aren't treated specially (Figma ignores the mask's position in flow the same way); PDF without the renderer clips alpha masks by their outline.

## Snapping, smart guides and drop into frames

Built 2026-10-19 in `apps/web/src/editor/snap/` and `camera/` (interactions.md §2 "Drop onto a frame", §4, §8).

- **Snapping** (`snap.ts`): a drag builds a `SnapIndex` once (`candidates.ts`: the moving layers' siblings and parent, plus the children of the frame they would drop into), with every edge and centre in sorted arrays; each pointer move is a binary search per edge, so 12,000 candidates cost well under a millisecond per move (unit test: index built under 200 ms, a move under 8 ms; measured far lower). Edges and centres snap within 4 screen pixels (`SNAP_PX`, divided by the zoom). Equal spacing: a box between two neighbours in a row or column snaps to the centre of the gap, or one existing gap after its neighbour; pills show each gap. With nothing in reach, positions land on whole pixels (the pixel grid). Resizing snaps the moving edges (upright layers, not with ⌥); drawing snaps both corners. Ctrl (or ⌘) held suspends snapping (pixel grid only). Guides are 1 px `guide/smart` lines across the layers that line up; pills are `guide/smart` with `accent/on` text (`overlay.ts`, DOM, never scaled by zoom).
- **Drop onto a frame** (`drop.ts`): while moving, the innermost visible frame (frame, component, section) under the pointer that is larger than the moving layers in both directions gets a 2 px `accent/default` outline (`.drop-target`); on release the layers become its children with their page position and angle kept, in the same undo step as the move. ⌘ drops a larger layer into an auto layout frame. Inside auto layout the insertion line (the reorder code's `reorderSlot`) shows where they go. Space keeps the current parent. Dragging out of a frame onto empty canvas puts layers on the page; children of an auto layout frame dragged out of it (past the reorder reach) drop onto the page or the frame under the pointer. Layers inside groups stay in their group. Frames never drop into themselves.
- **⌥-drag** duplicates: the copies (made with stable ids inside the live edit) move and are selected, the originals stay and can be snapped to; one undo step ("Duplicate layer"). Escape puts the selection back on the originals.
- **⌘D repeat** (`repeat.ts`): after a duplicate (⌘D or ⌥-drag) whose copies were then moved, ⌘D with the copies selected places the next copies at the same offset again.
- **⌥ measurements**: ⌥ held with a selection, hovering another layer shows the distances between them (gaps across, or edge to edge when they overlap); over nothing, to the parent frame's edges. ⌘⌥ measures to nested layers (deep hit-test). Pressing or letting go of ⌥ updates it without moving the pointer.
- **Zoom** (`camera/zoom.ts`): ⇧0 and ⌘0 100%, ⇧1 fit all, ⇧2 zoom to the selection, ⌘+ and ⌘− step by powers of two (75% → 100% → 200%), ⌘/Ctrl + wheel and trackpad pinch zoom around the pointer; a plain wheel now pans (Shift + wheel sideways), as in Figma. The zoom readout stays in the title bar (click: fit).
- **Settings** (`settings.ts`): "Snap to objects" is read from this browser's storage (`illigma.snapToObjects` = `off`) until the View menu exists; the e2e helpers turn it off for scripts that check exact positions (`newContext(browser, { snap: true })` keeps it).
- **Tests**: `snap/snap.test.ts` (25: nearest candidate, edge, centre and pixel snapping, suspension, equal spacing centred and repeated, coordinates and points, 12,000-candidate timing, measurements, drop targets (fit, ⌘ into auto layout, Space, never into itself), reparenting keeps page position and angle in one undo step, out to the page, auto layout slot, groups kept, ⌘D repeat offset, candidates, zoom steps, zoom at a point, fit). E2E `snap.mjs` (43 checks): edge snap with a guide, Ctrl suspends, equal spacing with 75 px pills, resize snapping, ⌥ measurement, drop into Desktop with the outline and one undo, a larger layer never drops, Space keeps the parent, dragging Hero out of Desktop, ⌥-drag and ⌘D repeat, zoom shortcuts, ⇧2, Ctrl + wheel and wheel panning. `canvas.mjs`: an auto layout child dragged far out now leaves the card (one undo puts it back).
- **Not yet:** snapping to layout grids, rulers' guides and points of vector paths; the View menu and ⌘K snapping toggles (storage only for now); a zoom presets menu on the readout (the View menu design has one); the pixel grid drawn at 400% and ⇧⌘' / ⌘' toggles; distance pills to the parent when an edge snaps to it (pills show for equal spacing and ⌥ measuring); snapping turned layers' own edges (their upright bounds snap); ⌥ toggled mid-drag doesn't switch between move and duplicate (only ⌥ at the start of the drag counts); ⌥-dragging children of an auto layout frame reorders instead of duplicating; Safari's `gesturechange` pinch.

## Canvas text

Text editing on the canvas (core-loop.md rows "Canvas: text editing" and "Text selected: typography card, text styles, font picker"; interactions.md §6; Figma boards "Text selected · font picker open" `96:58050`, "Card / Typography" `96:58849`, "Font picker" `96:59007`).

- **Format** (format 0, optional, Figma Plugin API names): `textAutoResize` (`NONE`, `WIDTH_AND_HEIGHT`, `HEIGHT`, `TRUNCATE`; absent is `NONE`, because format 0 drew text wrapped in a fixed box; the editor sets it on the layers it creates), `textAlignHorizontal` (`LEFT`, `CENTER`, `RIGHT`, `JUSTIFIED`; absent `LEFT`), `textAlignVertical` (`TOP`, `CENTER`, `BOTTOM`; absent `TOP`), `letterSpacing` (`{ value, unit: "PIXELS" | "PERCENT" }`, value within ±10,000), and `lineHeight` now also takes Figma's `{ value, unit: "PIXELS" | "PERCENT" }` and `{ unit: "AUTO" }` (a bare number is pixels, as in format 0). Helpers `lineHeightPx` and `letterSpacingPx`. `Transaction.create` takes an optional id (a live edit that creates a node keeps one id).
- **Text engine** (`TextEngine` in `@illigma/renderer`, `text.ts`): one font provider and one paragraph builder for drawing, export, layout and editing, so a box layout sized never wraps differently on the canvas. `place` (wrap at the box, alignment in it, vertical alignment, auto width on one line per line break, truncation with "…"), `measure` (auto width and auto height), `layout` (`TextLayout`: lines, caret, selection rectangles, hit-testing, grapheme steps from CanvasKit's glyph info). SkParagraph rounds line widths up to whole pixels before wrapping, so auto widths are whole pixels. `addFonts` registers fonts later (fonts on this computer); the renderer re-records and listeners draw again. `PageRenderer` takes `textEngine` (shared, the app's) or `fonts` (its own, as before).
- **Layout** (`@illigma/layout`): `relayout`, `resize` and `resizeMany` take `measureText`. Auto width text hugs its text; auto height wraps at its width (or the Fill width) and hugs its height; fixed and truncated text keep their box; text that can't be measured yet (no fonts) keeps its stored size. Text fields count as layout changes (`LAYOUT_KEYS`). Auto width text grows from its alignment, as in Figma (centred both ways, right-aligned to the left). In the app, the layout normalizer measures with the app's text engine once it has loaded (`text/engine.ts`, `layoutMeasure`). Text in a font that isn't loaded (not installed, or not read yet) is never measured (`TextEngine.measure` returns null for an unregistered family): it keeps the size stored in the file, as Figma keeps text in a missing font, so moving it, its parent or a sibling in its auto layout never rewrites it in Inter. And resizing text by its handles or W and H follows Figma: a new width makes auto width text auto height, a new height makes it a fixed size. The W and H sizing menus offer Hug for text and map Hug, Fixed and Fill to the resize mode.
- **Text tool and editing** (`apps/web/src/editor/text/`): T (or the dock) then click makes an auto width layer (Inter Regular 12, black) with its first line centred on the point; a drag makes a fixed-size box; both start editing at once, inside the frame under the pointer (so a click in an auto layout frame adds to its flow). A click with the tool on a text layer edits it. Double-click on a text layer edits with the caret at the point; Enter on a selected text layer edits with all text selected; a click on another text layer while editing moves there; Escape (or a click elsewhere, Tab, another tool, the selection changing in Layers) leaves editing with the layer selected. Empty text is removed when editing ends. The caret (2 px) and selection (accent at 32%) are DOM overlays placed from the renderer's line layout, so they follow zoom and rotation. Keyboard input goes through a hidden `<textarea>` (the focus target, named "Edit text: <layer>"): typing, Backspace and Delete (word and line deletion too), plain-text copy, cut and paste, and IME composition (shown in the layer while composing) are the browser's; ← → (grapheme), ⌥← ⌥→ (word, Ctrl elsewhere), ↑ ↓ (keeping the column), ⌘← ⌘→ and Home/End (visual line), ⌘↑ ⌘↓ and Ctrl+Home/End (text), with Shift to extend, ⌘A, double-click (word) and triple-click (paragraph), drag-select. The layer is named after its first line until it is renamed.
- **Undo steps** (`text/session.ts`): a new layer and what is typed until the first pause are one step, "Add text"; leaving a new layer empty leaves no step. Then each typing burst is one step ("Type text", "Delete text", "Paste text"): a burst ends after 1 s without a key, when the caret moves or the selection changes, when typing turns into deleting or back, on paste or cut (a step of their own), when an IME composition is committed, on undo, and when focus leaves the text (a panel, another app). ⌘Z while editing ends the burst, then undoes one step; undoing the layer's creation leaves editing. A burst also ends after 2 s of continuous typing or composing (`BURST_MAX_MS`), so typing reaches the recovery journal at least that often (the PRD's "last 2 s recoverable"); the caret stays. A burst that deletes all of a layer's text stays open until text is typed again or editing ends, so leaving the emptied layer removes it in that same "Delete text" step (one undo brings it back with its text). Bursts are live edits (`Document.beginLive`), so the canvas and layout follow every key; anything else that edits ends the burst first. A new layer with no text is never kept: not on blur (focus in a panel), not when a save settles open edits, not when an IME composition starts; it stays an open live edit (so it is neither saved nor journaled, and saving waits while it is open), and leaving it, reloading, closing the tab or Back to files removes it with no undo step (`settleTextForSave`). Typography changes made to it before typing go into its "Add text" step (`patchNew`); other panels wait until something is typed. An IME composition in a new layer is one "Add text" step. Editor shortcuts with ⌘ (Ctrl elsewhere) reach the editor while typing: ⌘S (the burst is kept, then saved), ⇧⌘E, ⌘= ⌘- ⌘0, and ⌘D and ⌘P do nothing rather than the browser's bookmark and print; the text input keeps clipboard and word or line deletion keys, and Ctrl+letter on macOS.
- **Typography card** (Properties, for text layers): font family (font picker), style (the family's styles; Inter's nine weights), size (with Figma's presets), line height (Auto, px or %; typing "auto" works; switching units keeps the spacing), letter spacing (px or %, converted when switching), horizontal alignment, resize mode (auto width, auto height, fixed size) and vertical alignment. Several text layers are edited together; values that differ show "Mixed". Each change is one undo step. Accessible names never say "width" or "height" (the W and H fields own those words): the resize buttons are "Grow horizontally", "Grow vertically" and "Fixed size" with Figma's names as tooltips, and line height is "Line spacing".
- **Font picker**: search, All / In this file / On this computer, fonts the file uses, the bundled Inter, and fonts on this computer through `queryLocalFonts()` (Chromium) after a click (the browser asks for permission; when it was granted before, the list is read on its own). A family's files are read only when it is picked or named by text in the file: when permission was granted before, the file's families on this computer are read as the file opens (`text/file-fonts.ts`), not only when a text layer is selected. Their text is then drawn in its font; sizes are not rewritten on their own when a font arrives (the next edit that reaches the text measures it). A font picked while another edit runs (typing, a drag) is applied when that edit ends, or the editor says why it couldn't. A family the file names that isn't available is flagged on the card and in the picker ("<font> is not installed"; the text is drawn in Inter until a font is picked). Keyboard: type to search, ↑ ↓, Enter, Escape (back to the font button).
- **Bounds**: `fontSize` above 0 and at most `MAX_TEXT_METRIC` (10,000); a numeric (format 0) `lineHeight` from 0 to 10,000. Files written before these bounds open with out-of-range values clamped (`clampTextMetrics`, on read, with a warning; format 0, so no migration step) instead of being refused; the text engine clamps what it lays out, and layout never stores a size that isn't finite.
- **Export**: PNG, JPG and PDF use the renderer, so they pick up every field. Fonts on this computer the text uses are sent to the export worker with the snapshot; a family that isn't available is drawn in Inter, with a warning.
- **Weights**: the text engine sets the `wght` axis from the style (`fontVariations`), so the bundled variable Inter draws each of its nine weights for real (before, every style drew the default instance with synthetic bold); fonts without the axis ignore it.
- **Keyboard**: Enter and Space on a focused control (button, menu item, radio, switch…) are the control's, never the editor's (Enter editing the selected text, Space panning); Space on a button focused by a click still pans when the pointer is over the canvas (`focus.ts`: the last pointer press tells a clicked button from one reached by the keyboard). Single-key shortcuts are ignored inside dialogs, popovers and menus. Layer names from text are cut between graphemes (`cutGraphemes`).
- **Number fields** (`ui/NumberField.tsx`, every inspector field): a number or simple maths (`+ − × ÷`, `*`, `/`, "x" between numbers, parentheses, a decimal comma, a trailing px or %); a leading `+`, `*` or `/` is relative to each layer's own value, so it works on Mixed (a leading − is a negative number, as in Figma). Anything else is refused with "… isn't a number" rather than taking its first number. SVG takes line positions from the renderer when fonts are given (alignment included) and writes `letter-spacing`; without fonts it anchors lines with `text-anchor` (middle, end) and places them by vertical alignment and the line height in any unit.
- **Tests**: `packages/renderer/src/text.test.ts` (10: auto width never wraps at its measured width, wrapping, empty text, letter spacing and line height units, alignment, truncation, caret and hit-testing round trips, trailing line breaks, graphemes, fonts added later), `packages/layout/src/text.test.ts` (6), `packages/format/src/text-fields.test.ts` (4), `packages/export/src/text-fields.test.ts` (3), `packages/core/src/create-id.test.ts` (2), `apps/web/src/editor/text/text.test.ts` (20: selection model, undo steps, placement, card values, sizing). E2E `apps/web/e2e/text.mjs` (114 checks): click and drag creation, typing, ⌥⇧← and double-click word selection, Shift+arrows, triple-click, copy and paste, Backspace, undo and redo per burst, empty text removed, wrapping and Enter in a fixed box, auto width growing, font size and alignment, the font picker, Hug text reflowing the Card, IME composition through CDP, reload, axe with no violations while editing, on the card and in the picker; QC round 8: ⌘S, ⌘D, ⌘P, ⌘0, ⌘=, ⌘- and ⇧⌘E while editing (prevented, and acting), an empty new layer gone after panel focus and reload, after Back to files, and after undoing an IME composition, a 2 s burst cap, Enter and Space on focused buttons, number field maths, and an emptied layer coming back in one undo; `export.mjs` adds Cancel while writing (stops between files, a neutral note, Export kept, reset on reopen). Also `text/text.test.ts` (emptied layers), `ui/NumberField.test.ts` (typed maths), the renderer weight test (variable Inter vs static Black, pixels and widths). QC round 8 unit tests: `apps/web/src/editor/text/qc8.test.ts` (7), `packages/format/src/text-metrics.test.ts` (3), and additions to the renderer, layout and export text tests, `export-files.test.ts` and `export/model.test.ts`.
- **Not yet**: mixed styles inside one text (character ranges: ⌘B, ⌘I, ⌘U do nothing yet), text styles, paragraph spacing, lists, decoration and case, OpenType features, ⌘⇧> and ⌘⇧< sizes; double-clicking a text box edge to return to Auto (dragging an edge already sets auto height or fixed size, as in Figma); Figma's drill-in on double-click (it edits the deepest text under the pointer at once); bundled fonts beyond Inter (no CJK, so Japanese, Chinese and Korean draw as boxes unless a local font is picked; no system fallback); a font loaded later doesn't re-measure auto width text until an edit reaches it (deliberately: the stored size is the font's own); editing text in a missing font is allowed (Figma blocks it) and keeps its box; italic for Inter (only upright is bundled); gradient and image text fills are offset by vertical alignment in a fixed box; right-to-left text caret movement; the picker's "Replace…" across the whole file.

## Vector tools

- **Code**: `apps/web/src/editor/vector/` (`network.ts` pure network maths, `ops.ts` document edits, `VectorController.ts` canvas pen/line/arrow/edit mode with an SVG overlay, `shortcuts.ts`, `VectorCards.tsx`, `store.ts`); `packages/renderer/src/shape-extras.ts` (corner rounding, arrow heads, operands) and `path-ops.ts` (CanvasKit PathOps, stroke outline; `PageRenderer` registers it with `usePathOps`).
- **Format**: `BOOLEAN_OPERATION` node type and `booleanOperation` (`UNION` default, `SUBTRACT`, `INTERSECT`, `EXCLUDE`); stroke caps `ARROW_LINES`, `ARROW_EQUILATERAL`; `VectorVertex.strokeCap` overrides the node cap at a free end. The Arrow tool makes a two-point VECTOR whose end vertex has `ARROW_LINES` (a LINE has one cap for both ends).
- **Booleans**: children are operands (bottom first; subtract takes the rest from the bottom one). The result is computed per frame-change and cached on the node and its subtree identities; without a PathOps engine (pure Node code) the operands draw unmerged. Hit-testing returns the boolean, never its operands.
- **Gaps**: the boolean's box isn't re-fitted when an operand moves later (only when made); PDF export skips equilateral arrow heads; no pen-on-existing-path continuation, no snapping to geometry while drawing, no pencil; vector edit handles are shown for selected points only; booleans have no keyboard shortcuts. Icons for booleans, flatten and outline are hand-written Lucide-style paths; the flyout and cards have no approved Figma design yet.

## Vector geometry

- **Fields** (format v0, optional, Figma Plugin API names): `vectorNetwork` on VECTOR (`vertices` `{ x, y, cornerRadius? }`, `segments` `{ start, end, tangentStart, tangentEnd }` with tangents relative to their vertex, `regions` `{ windingRule: "NONZERO" | "EVENODD", loops: number[][] }`), in the node's own space (0…width, 0…height); `pointCount` (POLYGON, default 3; STAR, default 5; whole numbers from 3 to 1,000); `innerRadius` (STAR, 0–1, default 0.382); `strokeCap` (`NONE` default, `ROUND`, `SQUARE`); `strokeJoin` (`MITER` default, `BEVEL`, `ROUND`). Absent fields mean Figma's defaults; files without them save byte for byte as before.
- **Validation** (`validate.ts`, also used by every `Document.transact`): exact keys, finite numbers, integer indices in range, every region loop a chain of segments that join end to end and close (in either direction), caps of 100,000 vertices, 100,000 segments, 10,000 regions and 200,000 loop entries.
- **Helpers** (`@illigma/format`, `vector.ts`): `vectorNetworkFromSvgPath(d, { windingRule })`, `svgPathFromVectorNetwork(net)`, `vectorNetworkProblem`, `walkVectorLoop`, `vectorStrokeChains` (segments joined into runs through points shared by exactly two segments), `vectorNetworkPaths` (flat path commands per region and for the stroke), `arcToCubics`. Bad input only ever throws `FormatError`; a fuzz test checks 3,000 random and mutated paths.
- **Drawing** (`geometry.ts` in `@illigma/renderer`, shared with hit-testing): a vector fills each region with its winding rule (no regions: its runs fill as if closed, as in Figma and SVG) and strokes all segments with caps and joins; polygons and stars sit on the ellipse inscribed in the box, first point at the top centre, corners rounded by `cornerRadius`; a line strokes from (0, 0) to (width, 0). Geometry is cached per network, so moving a vector doesn't rebuild it, and the per-screen picture caching is unchanged.
- **Hit-testing**: polygons and stars by shape; vectors by filled regions when they have a visible fill and by their stroke when they have a visible stroke or no fill; lines by stroke. Strokes reach half their weight plus `tolerance` (default 2 world units; `PageRenderer.hitTest` passes 3 screen pixels), plus square caps and miter joins as drawn (miter limit 4). On closed shapes, inside strokes reach their whole weight inward only and outside strokes their whole weight outward only, as drawn (QC round 7); rectangles, ellipses and frames with a centred or outside stroke can be picked on the part of the stroke past their box (a frame still clips its children to its box). Curves are flattened to a 0.05-unit tolerance (more for paths over 500 units), at most 250,000 points per path, and cached.
- **Resizing** (QC round 6): whenever layout changes a vector's size (handles, W and H, constraints, Fill and Hug), its network is scaled to the new size in the same undo step.
- **SVG open subpaths**: SVG fills an open subpath as if closed, without stroking the closing line. A region can't do that, because its loops are made of stroked segments. So a path whose open subpaths enclose an area gets no region, and every run fills as if closed with the nonzero rule, as for paths with no closed subpath. Exception (QC round 7): with the even-odd rule and subpaths whose boxes overlap (holes), the closed subpaths keep their even-odd region and the open ones go unfilled. An open subpath encloses an area only when its shoelace area (curves sampled) isn't zero, so lines along one line or back over themselves don't count.
- **Import**: prototype paths (SVG `d` in a `baseW` × `baseH` box) become networks scaled to the layer, even-odd, with round caps and joins when stroked.
- **Benchmark** (10,000 layers, now including 1,300 stars, polygons and curved vectors with holes; Apple M1, ANGLE Metal, 2× pixel density): median 60 fps, 99.3–99.7% of frames within 16.7 ms, 4.1–4.9 ms draw per frame, page recorded once in 90–96 ms. With one edit per frame (`--edit`): 100% within budget. Before this change (rectangles, ellipses and text only): 100%, 4.5 ms, 87 ms.
- **Not yet:** per-vertex `cornerRadius` and node `cornerRadius` on vectors are stored but not drawn; arrow caps; per-region fills; `LINE` arrowheads; `ELLIPSE` arc data; the vector editor itself.

## Paints and effects

- **Paints** (format 0, Figma Plugin API names): `SOLID`; `GRADIENT_LINEAR`, `GRADIENT_RADIAL`, `GRADIENT_ANGULAR` and `GRADIENT_DIAMOND`, each with `gradientStops` (`{ position, color }`, 1 to 1,000) and `gradientTransform` (`[[a, c, e], [b, d, f]]`, mapping the node's box normalised to 0…1 into gradient space). In gradient space, linear runs from (0, 0.5) to (1, 0.5); radial, angular and diamond are centred on (0.5, 0.5); angular starts towards (1, 0.5) and runs clockwise. `IMAGE` has `imageHash` (SHA-256, or `null`), `scaleMode` (`FILL`, `FIT`, `CROP` or `TILE`), `imageTransform` (CROP: box to image, both normalised) and `scalingFactor` (TILE: tile size as a multiple of the image's pixels). Every paint can have `opacity` (default 1) and `blendMode` (default `NORMAL`). Unknown keys are refused. Image `filters` and `rotation` aren't supported yet.
- **Effects and compositing** (node fields): `effects` (bottom first, at most 100) can hold `DROP_SHADOW` and `INNER_SHADOW` (`color`, `offset`, `radius` = CSS blur = 2σ, `spread`, `blendMode`, plus `showShadowBehindNode` on drop shadows, default false) and `LAYER_BLUR` and `BACKGROUND_BLUR` (`radius`). `blendMode` takes all 19 of Figma's modes; absent means `PASS_THROUGH`. `strokeAlign` is `CENTER`, `INSIDE` or `OUTSIDE`; absent means `CENTER`, because format 0 drew every stroke centred. Figma's default for new shapes is `INSIDE`, so the editor should set it on the layers it creates. Lines and open paths are always stroked centred. `isMask` is not in the format yet (see below).
- **Images in the package**: `images/<hash>.<ext>` holds PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF files of at most 32 MB, 16,384 pixels a side and 64 megapixels (read from the header by `imageDimensions`, before decoding), named by the SHA-256 of their bytes. `FileTree` is now path → `string | Uint8Array`: everything under `images/` is bytes, everything else is text. Saving writes every image the file holds (used ones first), and refuses unknown formats, oversize files and hash mismatches. Opening never fails because of an image: a damaged, unknown or oversize image is left out with a warning. A missing image gets a warning and draws as a faint grey. Unused or unrecognised files under `images/` are ignored, with a warning; `ReadReport.unusedImages` lists the sound unused ones, the only image files a host may remove. `readPackageWithReportAsync` hashes images with `crypto.subtle` first (the web app opens files through it); the synchronous reader stays for Node and tests. Zips store images uncompressed.
- **Image files on disk** (QC round 7, rules in [save-states.md](../design/save-states.md#images-qc-round-7-18-october-2026)): saves never remove image files; an image already on disk is written again only if its size differs or (once per session) its bytes don't match its name. The web app removes sound unused image files only when a file opens with no recovered edits pending (`Backend.collectImages`). The recovery journal copies every added image (`Document.subscribeImages`) before the change lines that use it, and Keep adds them back.
- **Drawing**: paints draw in the node's space through CanvasKit shaders (the diamond gradient through a runtime effect). An image is decoded once, after the recording that first needs it; then only the top-level layers that use it are re-recorded (chunked frames included), and `onImageReady` asks for a frame (the editor's canvas passes it). Decoded images no cached picture uses any more are freed after each recording. Shapes, and frames with a fill, cast shadows from their outline. Rectangles, rounded rectangles and ellipses use a blurred-box shader (exact along straight edges, close at corners), so the cost doesn't change with zoom. Other paths use Skia's blur mask. Groups, text and frames without a fill cast shadows from their pixels, through the layer's image filter. Inner shadows are clipped to the shape. Background blur is a backdrop filter clipped to the outline, drawn before the layer. Opacity, blend mode, layer blur and pixel-based shadows share one layer. A `NORMAL` frame isolates children that blend; `PASS_THROUGH` lets them blend with what is under it.
- **QC round 7, tester fixes** (2026-10-19, `spread.ts` in `@illigma/renderer`):
  - *Pixel effects on the CPU.* Shadow spread on groups, text and frames without a fill used Skia's morphology filters, which on CPU surfaces (export, the CPU fallback canvas) cost about a microsecond per pixel per pixel of spread. A dilation by R device pixels is now about log2(2R) passes, each the maximum of the image and a copy shifted by 1, 2, 4, … pixels (the alpha moved into opaque grey, where `Lighten` is the maximum; erosion uses `Darken` and keeps only pixels both copies cover), each pass a `Compose` so nothing is computed twice. It matches `MakeDilate` pixel for pixel. When the passes would still cost more than 1.5 million pixel-passes, or the blur sigma is 8 device pixels or more, the work runs on a copy reduced by powers of two (exact 2 × 2 averages; spread reduces at most 16x, blur at most 64x, sigma staying at least 4 reduced pixels). Layers with effects record what they draw first and are bounded by it. Drop shadows are drawn under the layer, knocked out (DstOut) only inside its bounds; inner shadows are the shadow colour where the layer draws less the blurred, moved layer, laid over it with SrcATop. Device scale comes from the canvas for export, and from the pixel density and the zoom rounded to a power of two for recordings: on the CPU, top-level layers with these effects are re-recorded when that changes. On the GPU, Skia's own filters are kept. 2000 × 2000 CPU surface: group shadow with spread 300, 72 s → 30 ms (300 ms at zoom 4); radius, spread and offset 1000, about 180 s → 0.4 s; layer blur 1000, 250 ms; PNG export at 4x with spread 50, 35.7 s → 0.6 s; PDF with spread 100, 17.2 s → 0.3 s. A layer that covers the whole surface costs about 240 ms in CanvasKit's CPU build whatever it draws, so effects covering all of it stay around 300–400 ms.
  - *Very large images.* Images over 2048 × 2048 pixels, on the GPU in a browser, are decoded with `createImageBitmap` (off the main thread) and uploaded once as a texture (`Surface.makeImageFromTextureSource`). Mipmaps on a texture would be rebuilt by Skia on every draw, so the renderer makes half-size copies on the GPU when first needed and draws the one closest to the drawn size (within 1.4x), without mipmaps; top-level layers using them are re-recorded when the zoom crosses a power of two. `GpuCanvas` raises the GPU resource cache budget to 768 MB. 8000 × 8000 fill, panning: p50 100 → 16.7 ms, p95 133 → 16.8 ms; longest task while opening 366–813 → about 50 ms (e2e check in `perf.mjs`).
  - *Text strokes* are drawn over the fills: centred as they are; inside and outside at twice the weight in a layer cut to the glyphs (DstIn or DstOut) drawn solid.
  - *Images added later*: `RenderSource.subscribeImages` (a `Document` has it) tells the renderer about new images; layers that drew one as missing are re-recorded and `onImageReady` asks for a frame.
  - `layoutText` lines also have `ascent`, `descent` and `width` (optional in `TextLine`).
  - *Open warnings*: `ReadReport.warnings` (damaged, missing or oversized images, stray files) reach `Session.warnings`, and the editor shows them in a dismissable banner, the first three listed (`openWarnings`).
  - *E2E harness*: a step watchdog in `runE2E` (`E2E_STEP_TIMEOUT`, default 120 s scaled by `T()`) fails a stalled script with its step and a screenshot; `run-all.mjs` runs each script in its own process group and stops it, browser included, after `E2E_SUITE_TIMEOUT` (default 10 minutes); browser and server shutdown are bounded. Three full runs at once and runs with `E2E_SLOW=1` passed without a stall; the reopen loop (`Back to files` and reopen with an 8000 × 8000 image, 60 cycles, and 40 with `E2E_SLOW=1`) didn't hang. The likeliest cause of the earlier stalls was the per-frame re-upload of large images keeping the main thread and GPU busy; any stall now fails fast with its step instead.
  - Tests: `qc7-tester.test.ts` (14: pixels against Skia's own filters for grown, shrunk and inner shadows; a huge spread at reduced resolution; timing budgets; zoom re-recording; text strokes; `layoutText`; image level choice; an image added after its paint), `open-warnings.test.ts` (3), one in `files-images.test.ts`.
- **Benchmark**: the scene now adds a soft shadow on all 100 screens, 600 linear and 900 radial gradient fills, 400 small drop shadows and 400 inner shadows (`--plain` gives the old scene; `--fx=screens,gradients,shadows,inner` picks families). Apple M1, ANGLE Metal, 2× pixel density: 97.6–98.7% of frames within 16.7 ms, 6.5–7.0 ms draw per frame, recorded once in 146–160 ms. With one edit per frame: 97.3%, 1.6 ms per edit. The plain scene: 100%, 4.8 ms, 106 ms (before this change: 100%, 4.8 ms, 102 ms). At four times the effect density (3,200 gradients, 700 small and 700 inner shadows), the scene is at the 95% gate: 94.7–95.4%, 7.5 ms draw. The first version, with Skia masks for every shadow, measured 79 ms per frame there. Effect-heavy files will need tile caching later.
- **Not yet:** on-canvas gradient handles (the editor panels are below); `isMask` (masking siblings doesn't fit the one-picture-per-top-level-layer cache, so it needs its own design); background blur on groups and text; effect `blendMode` on pixel-based shadows (groups, text); off-thread decoding of images up to 2048 × 2048 and of all images on the CPU (they decode on the main thread, between frames); image `filters` and `rotation`; noise, texture, progressive blur and glass (P1/P2); prototype CSS gradient strings (the prototype never saved gradient fills, so they stay unread with a warning). ## Export

`@illigma/export` (MPL-2.0) turns layers into files. It never touches the disk and has no Node-only code, so it runs in the web app, a worker or Node; the app decides where the bytes go (the folder, or a `.zip` in browsers without folder access, per [the export design](../design/export.md)).

- **API.** `exportNode(source, nodeId, settings, deps?)` → `{ filename, mime, bytes, width, height, warnings }` (Figma's `exportAsync`). `exportNodes(source, requests, deps?, { taken, onProgress })` → `{ files, failures }` for the Export dialog: one failure (hidden layer, too big) doesn't stop the rest, and each failure says why, ready for **Retry**. `exportPdf(source, nodeIds, deps?)` writes one PDF with a page per layer. `planExportFilenames(source, requests, taken)` lists the names a batch will write, for the dialog. `source` is anything with `get` and `children` (a `Document` is one). `deps` is `{ canvasKit, fonts, limits }`; SVG and PDF work without CanvasKit, except for text in PDFs.
- **Settings** (Figma's `ExportSettings`): `format` PNG, JPG, SVG or PDF; `constraint` `{ type: SCALE | WIDTH | HEIGHT, value }` (PNG and JPG; SVG and PDF are 1x, as in Figma); `suffix`; `contentsOnly` (default true; false draws everything on the page inside the layer's bounds); `useAbsoluteBounds` (default false: the bounds of what the layer draws, strokes and clipping included; true: its box); `quality` 0–100 for JPG (default 90); SVG `svgIdAttribute`, `svgOutlineText`, `svgSimplifyStroke`. Bad settings throw `ExportError` with a plain-language message.
- **File names**, as Figma makes them: `<layer name><suffix>.<ext>`, with each `/` in the name starting a folder (`Icons/Arrow@2x.png`). Characters Windows or macOS refuse become `_`, control characters, trailing dots and spaces go, Windows device names get a `_`, `.`/`..` parts are dropped (no escaping the folder), and each part stays under 200 bytes. In a batch, a name that is taken (by an earlier file or by `taken`, compared without case) gets `-1`, `-2`… (`Welcome-1.png`), so nothing is replaced, as the design asks.
- **PNG and JPG** use the renderer's own drawing code (`PageRenderer.drawSubtree`, new) on an offscreen CPU surface, so exports match the canvas. Sizes are whole pixels, at least 1 × 1. PNG keeps transparency; JPG is drawn over white. The most an image can be is 16,384 px a side and 134 million pixels (`DEFAULT_EXPORT_LIMITS`, adjustable); past that the error says the size it would be and suggests a smaller scale. CanvasKit's default build can only encode PNG, so JPG uses CanvasKit's encoder when the full build is loaded and a small baseline JPEG encoder of our own otherwise (`jpeg.ts`, full-resolution colour, standard tables).
- **SVG** is written from the layer tree: `<g>` for containers (layer opacity as group `opacity`), frames clip with a `<clipPath>` of their (rounded) box, `<rect>`, `<ellipse>`, and `<path>` for vectors, stars, polygons and lines (one per filled region with its winding rule, one for the stroke), `translate()`/`rotate()` transforms, solid fills and strokes with opacity, caps and joins. Text is `<text>` with a `<tspan>` per line, at the line breaks and baselines the canvas uses (`PageRenderer.layoutText`, new) when fonts are given, else one line per line break with Inter's metrics. Output is deterministic and escaped (names, text, ids), with the `viewBox` from the bounds. `svgIdAttribute` writes layer names as unique ids.
- **PDF** is our own minimal PDF 1.7 writer (CanvasKit 0.42 has no PDF backend), one page per layer at 1 px = 1 pt: vector paths with solid fills and strokes (winding rule, caps, joins, alpha), layer opacity as transparency groups, frame clipping. Deterministic (no dates). Text is drawn by the renderer into an image (4 px per pt) with a soft mask, so it looks right but can't be selected yet; without CanvasKit and fonts, text is left out with a warning.
- **Warnings** list what a file leaves out or approximates (no fonts, text in PDF as an image, outline text not available), so the UI can say so.
- **Tests** (`packages/export`, 44, plus 2 in the renderer for the new API): file names, bounds, PNG pixels decoded with CanvasKit (scale, fixed width and height, rotation, strokes, clipping, opacity, `contentsOnly`, text), JPG (white background, quality, and the encoder against Skia's decoder), golden SVG strings checked for well-formed XML, golden PDF content streams with every cross-reference offset and stream length checked, multi-page PDF, text as image, batch names and partial failures, and the size guard. SVG and PDF output were also compared by eye with the PNG (Chromium for SVG, macOS Quartz for PDF).
- **Paints and effects in export** (2026-10-18, after the paints and effects work merged with export). PNG and JPG get everything through the renderer, image bytes included: `ExportSource.image` (a `Document` has it) feeds the renderer, and `drawSubtree` decodes images on the spot. Export bounds include drop shadows (offset, spread, 3σ) and layer blur, and strokes reach by their alignment.
  - **SVG**: `<linearGradient>` and `<radialGradient>` with `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` and the layer-space `gradientTransform`. Angular and diamond gradients are written as radial ones, with a warning. Images become a `<pattern>` (a box-sized tile, or the scaled tile for TILE) using one embedded `<image>` per file (data URI, `xlink:href`). Paint and layer blend modes become `mix-blend-mode` (LINEAR_DODGE is `plus-lighter`; LINEAR_BURN is written as multiply, with a warning). Drop shadows, inner shadows and layer blur go into one `<filter>` per layer, built from its pixels. Inside strokes are doubled and clipped; outside strokes are doubled and masked. Background blur and shadow blend modes get a warning.
  - **PDF**: gradients are shading patterns (axial, or radial for the other three types, with a warning) placed in the content stream's space, with stitched functions for several stops. Stops with different opacities get one average alpha and a warning. Images are image XObjects clipped to the shape: decoded by CanvasKit (RGB with a soft mask), or a JPEG embedded as it is without CanvasKit. PNG, GIF and WebP without CanvasKit get a warning. Blend modes are `/BM`. Inside and outside strokes are doubled and clipped. A layer with shadows or blurs is drawn by the renderer into an image (4 px per pt), with a warning; without CanvasKit its effects are left out, with a warning.
  - Checked visually: one showcase scene exported to PNG, SVG (rendered in Chromium) and PDF (rendered by macOS) looks the same in all three. Tests: 14 in `packages/export/src/paints.test.ts`.
- **QC round 7** (2026-10-18). JPEG Exif orientation is read from the header (`imageSize`: shown and stored size, orientation); SVG and PDF store the image as in the file with the tag cleared and turn it with `orientationMatrix`, as CanvasKit does. PDF embeds 8-bit grey and RGB JPEGs (baseline or progressive) as they are even with CanvasKit, and decodes other images only up to 16,384 px a side and 64 million pixels. Export bounds reach miter tips (miter limit 4) and square caps' corners. SVG keeps filters, opacity and layer blend off elements with a clip, mask or paint blend; isolates NORMAL groups whose children blend (PDF: `/I true` groups); casts shapes' shadows from their outline as opaque geometry; prefixes generated ids with a fingerprint of the file. Text that runs past its box exports whole: bounds come from the renderer's line layout (`textLinesBounds`: the box, the last line's descent, and glyphs a tight line height lifts above the top), for the region, PDF forms and effect images, the PDF text image and SVG filter regions. Groups, text and one-sided strokes keep `feMorphology`, with a warning. `exportPdf` leaves hidden layers out with a warning, failing only when all are hidden. `uniqueFilename` makes raw paths safe; "CON .png" is reserved; more invisible format characters are dropped. PNG, JPG and PDF warn about characters no loaded font has. Tests: 14 in `packages/export/src/qc7-tester.test.ts`.
